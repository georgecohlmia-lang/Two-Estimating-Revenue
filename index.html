01

Inventory the Signal

The first move — before any method

Separate the part of your business where you have signal from the part where you do not.

You have more signal than you think — past conversations, expressed interest, real activity, reference points from channels you already know. But signal is never evenly spread. One channel may have years of history; another may have none. Name them honestly here. The two halves get estimated by two different methods in the sections that follow.

Has Signal

A channel with real history — past activity, a known conversion pattern, reference points you can weight. This goes through the Weighted Pipeline (Section 02).

No Signal

A channel with no past data — nothing to weight, nothing to convert from. This goes through the Labeled Hypothesis (Section 03). Do not pretend it is an estimate.

Christine's worked example: her referral channel — eighteen oncology practices, four years of history — has signal. Her new direct-to-consumer channel has none. Same business, two methods.

03

The Labeled Hypothesis

For the channel with no signal

When you have no history, do not pretend. Build a hypothesis — and mark it as one.

For a channel with no past data, you do not have an estimate. Build a deliberate hypothesis instead: a modest traffic figure, a conservative conversion rate borrowed from published norms for your category, an average order value. A number you have honestly labeled as unproven is still useful — it gives you something to test against real results. A guess allowed to look like a forecast is dangerous, because no one knows to question it.

04

The Capacity Check

The step most owners skip

A revenue number assumes a level of activity. The colder question is whether the business can physically deliver it.

A forecast that beats your capacity is fiction, however good the math looks. Run all three checks below. For each one, give an honest verdict — and if the answer is anything but a clean yes, you fix the plan, not the spreadsheet.

CHECK 01 Sales Capacity

Your weighted pipeline assumes new opportunities open at a certain pace. Can your sales effort — the people and the hours you actually have — realistically open them that fast, given how long one relationship genuinely takes to build?

CHECK 02 Fulfilment Capacity

If both channels grow at once, the order volume rises with them. Can the business actually deliver, pack, and ship that volume at the quality your customers and referral relationships depend on?

CHECK 03 Owner Capacity

Every plan quietly assumes the owner can personally absorb it. Can you personally manage everything this estimate depends on — new hires, a new channel, the existing business — all in the same year?

05

The Three-Point Range

Conservative · Base · Upside

Do not collapse the estimate to one number. Build a range — and confirm the conservative number sustains the business.

Your weighted pipeline and your labeled hypothesis feed a base figure. From there, set a conservative floor and an upside ceiling. Then ask the question that matters most: can the business survive for six months on the conservative number? If not, the model has a structural problem — and you want to find it now, not in month three.

Conservative
Your planning floor. Low-end conversion, a longer sales cycle, a disruption or two. The number the business must be able to survive on.
Base
The midpoint, where capacity holds to plan. The number you manage to. Pulled directly from the sections above.
Upside
Ceiling awareness, not a target. Deals closing faster; the no-signal channel converting at the top of its hypothesis.
